Repair Or Replace

Can Your Chip Be Repaired?

It comes down to two things. How big the damage is, and where it sits on the screen. Everything else is detail.

What The MOT Allows

The MOT splits the screen into zones. Zone A is a 290mm wide strip inside the area the wipers sweep, centred on the steering wheel. Damage more than 10mm across in there is a fail. Anywhere else in the swept area the limit is 40mm. Testers are told to fail damage only where it significantly affects your view of the road.

Here is the part most people do not know. The inspection manual treats a repair that is finished flush with the glass, so it is effectively invisible, as not being damage at all. Fill a chip properly and it stops being an MOT problem.

If It Cannot Be Repaired

When The Screen Has To Come Out

Some damage is past filling. If any of this sounds like your screen, it is a replacement rather than a repair, and that part is our work.

1
A Crack Running To The Edge

Once damage reaches the bonded edge of the glass the screen has lost strength there. Filling it does not put that back.

2
Too Big, Or Sat In Your Eyeline

Over 10mm in the strip in front of the steering wheel, or over 40mm elsewhere in the swept area, and you are into new glass.

3
Several Breaks Close Together

Two chips within a few centimetres can be treated as one damaged area, which pushes it over the limit even though each looks small.

4
It Has Already Spread

Cold nights, hot demisters and a rough road turn a chip into a crack. Once it has run, the repair window has gone.
